How Find Us / Crowd Compass works

The whole thing, step by step — taken from the manuscript's own sections, not re-imagined. This is how the crowd's phones build a shared relative map and use it to find an SOS.

[EXP:NOT-RUN] — honest, not hidden. These stages are the design (the paper's math and protocol). The radio mesh has not yet traded real bytes (5/5 bridge verbs absent; 0 devices scanned). So not a single stage below is labeled "measured" — each is labeled design or open. You are reading the intended mechanics, spelled out.
1

Newcomer joins — the phone with no history (§2.1, RQ3)

A device arrives “mid-crisis” with no shared history, no anchor, no map. It listens. It does not need a permanent origin — because the system never has one (ADR-002 · no-permanent-anchor). The newcomer's first job is just: hear the gradient, then climb it. That's the whole “how a new person comes in” story — no account, no anchor, no install-queue.

2

The crowd becomes the infrastructure (§2.2, §4.3)

There is no beacon, router, or tower. The crowd is the infrastructure: every phone listens more than it shouts (the energy asymmetry, §13.4), and the collection of phones forms a graph. A phone never connects to a phone; phones only advertise and scan (GAP/adv, no GATT links). That's the whole radio model — no pairing, no connection queue, just packets.

3

The gradient gets built — who is closest to the goal (§5, §12)

Each phone that hears the goal (SOS finding, or the “find us” direction) advertises a gradient value — how far, in hops, that goal feels from here. Neighbors read it DENEMO and update. This climbs and corrects as the crowd moves (ADR-003 · geometry vs topology). The result is a distributed relative coordinate system — no anchor ever exists, but phones still agree where “toward” is.

4

SOS arrives — an emergency packet with a purpose (§9, §27/§13.3, RQ6)

An SOS is a directional question, not a broadcast that floods everything. It travels downhill the gradient toward the person who can receive; peers forward the call only while it still points toward a listener (§13.4 traffic classes). So: SOS comes in as a precious, gradient-steered, honest byte — not a gossip storm.

5

The mesh self-heals as the crowd moves (§12/ADR-003, RQ7, RQ11)

When a partition splits, dissolves, or merges, relative coordinates must re-converge. The paper's design has nodes agree on relative geometry continuously; when two partitions meet, their gradients are re-reconciled — “who is new here, which gradient do I believe” becomes the honest open question (RQ7, RQ20). Movement is expected; re-convergence is the design target.

6

The crowd answers “find us” — the whole group, not the individuated few (§1, §5.1)

Because everyone advertises a small gradient, the crowd itself becomes the answer: any phone can compute the shortest-direction toward the SOS or toward the group, undirected, no server. The group “comes in” the same way the single phone does — the graph is the infrastructure, so the big crowd is just a bigger graph.
