1

A new person comes — no history, no anchor (§2.1, newcomer RQ3)

A phone arrives "mid-crisis" with no shared past. It does not need to be told where it is — the system never installs an anchor (ADR-002). It only needs to listen for the gradient. That is the paper's core non-requirement: "no permanent anchor ever exists."

2

The crowd becomes the infrastructure (§2.2, §4.3)

No beacon, no router, no tower. The crowd is the infrastructure: each phone only advertises and scans (GAP/adv verbs, no GATT connection at all — the "no pairing ever" byte-atom). One hop = one phone radio-hears another within range. That's the whole radio model.

3

The gradient gets built — the "closer than I am" math (§5, §12)

Each phone repeats: "how many radio-hops am I from the goal?" — and whispers it louder to whoever is director. Neighbors lower their hop-count by one toward the goal and relay. This is gradient-as-distributed-geometry: the crowd builds a "downhill toward the goal" field with no anchor, using only relative hops (the RSSI-is-not-distance ADR-005 keeps it honest).

4

SOS arrives — a direction, not a flood (§9, §13.3, RQ6)

SOS is steered as a gradient: it flows down the established gradient toward the SOS radio, each hop choosing the neighbor with lower hops-to-SOS. It suppresses/floods only when the gradient is too stale (§12.3). Result: the crowd points "toward" the SOS with real routing, not a blind broadcast storm.

5

The mesh self-heals as the crowd moves (§12 / ADR-003, RQ7, RQ11)

When partitions split, dissolve, or merge, the relative coordinates re-converge — the paper's honesty keeps RQ7 (partition heal) and RQ11 (motion re-convergence) explicitly open until real radios prove them.

6

The whole crowd answers "find us" (§5.1, the group)

The gradient is per-phone but the answer is the group: every phone that hears "closer + closer" toward the goal knows the whole crowd's direction. §5.1 says it plainly — the crowd, not the single device, is the finding system.
